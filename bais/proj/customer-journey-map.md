### Persona name
---
**Kiri — “Design-conscious Brand Evaluator”**

## Primary user
---
**Primary user = the design-driven buyer evaluating whether OAC is “worth it.”**

- They’re trying to validate: **taste fit + craftsmanship/credibility + brand meaning** quickly.
## Role summary

---
**“Design-conscious brand evaluator”**  
Responsible for making a confident call: _Is this brand/product aligned with my taste and the price justified?_  
They encounter the gap when a site looks “generic ecommerce” and fails to express identity/craft—so they can’t form trust or emotional pull.  
Real-life recognizable as: “the person who cares about aesthetic + story and won’t buy unless the brand feels legit.”

## Primary goal
---
**Make a confident, low-regret decision about buying a premium item.**

- **Success (their perspective):** “I understand what this brand _is_, what makes the product special, and I feel good paying this price.”
- **Relief/confidence trigger:** Clear brand narrative + proof of quality + coherent visual identity + an easy path to compare items/collections.
- **Value-based (not feature-based):** Confidence + meaning + trust. This goal stays the same even if the solution isn’t a website.

## Context of use
---
- **When it shows up:** First-time visits—often **mobile**, often **between tasks**, often “two-minute browse” behavior.
- **Trigger moment:** They land from an ad/referral and immediately ask: _“What is this and why is it special?”_
- **What’s happening around them:** Multitasking, low patience, maybe comparing multiple brands/tabs, mild skepticism (ad-driven traffic).
- **Recurring vs occasional:** Recurring for self-buyers (collectors/repeat shoppers); occasional for gift buyers.

## Key pain points
---
- **Brand ambiguity:** The site doesn’t quickly answer “what makes OAC different?”
- **Credibility gap:** Premium price requires proof (materials, craft, process, social proof) without feeling salesy.
- **Overchoice without guidance:** If collections/themes aren’t legible, exploration becomes work.
- **Visual mismatch:** If the aesthetic isn’t immediately compelling, they bounce (especially from social traffic).
- **Fear of regret:** Wrong decision = wasted money, bad gift, or disappointment vs expectations → anxiety + tab-close.

## Constraints
---
- **Time:** Short sessions; “decide fast or leave.”
- **Cognitive load:** They won’t read walls of text unless the site earns it through design + structure.
- **Access limits:** They can’t touch/feel the product; photos + narrative must carry the weight.
- **Competing priorities:** Comparing alternatives, price sensitivity, gift deadlines.
- **Unrealistic assumptions to avoid:**
    - Assuming they’ll explore deep pages by default
    - Assuming they trust “premium” claims without evidence
    - Assuming they’ll tolerate slow loads/heavy interactions for “cool design”
