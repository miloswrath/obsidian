Here are some **brief feedback ideas** you can use that focus only on story, detail, usage, and dialogue (not grammar/spelling):

---

### Overall Story
The story is clear and meaningful but it reads sort of like a summary or report rather than a personal narrative. Adding more emotion from your father's experiences (fear, confusion, relief) would go a long way to making it more engaging.
The timeline is easy to follow but it feels rushed at some times. Slowing down and emphasizing key turning points with details and emotion would help.
    

### Details

Some moments feel very general (e.g., “he was overcome with emotions”). Instead, try showing what emotions looked like—was he scared, frustrated, hopeful? What did his body language reveal?
More sensory details would help: what did the waiting room feel like? What did he notice about the doctors’ faces, the sounds, or his own physical state?
    

### Dialogue

- Dialogue is minimal and mostly functional (“go lay down,” “just go to acute care”). It could feel more real if you expanded it slightly—what exact words might your mom or the doctor have said that stuck with your dad?
    
- Right now, the dialogue mostly reports information. To sound more realistic, it could include tone, hesitation, or urgency (e.g., “We need to move you now” vs. “He was told to move to the ER”).
    

### Usage & Realism

- The narrative often tells what happened instead of showing it. Shifting to more “showing” through details and dialogue would make the story feel more immediate and less like a medical report.
    
- The ending is strong in summarizing long-term outcomes, but it might land better if you tied it back to the emotional weight of the experience—how it changed your father or your family’s view of health.
