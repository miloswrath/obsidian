## 1\. Introduction

White matter lesions (WMLs) are a hallmark feature of [vascular dementia](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/vascular-dementia). However, WMLs are, albeit to a lesser extent, also observed in cognitively normal older persons (, ) and patients with [cognitive deficits](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/cognitive-disorders) ranging from [mild cognitive impairment](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/mild-cognitive-impairment) (MCI) () to [Alzheimer's dementia](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/alzheimers-disease) (AD) (, ). Previous studies suggest that especially periventricular WMLs (pvWMLs) are associated with cognitive deficits (,,, ), the rate of cognitive decline (), and an increased risk of progression from MCI to AD (,,, ).

It has been hypothesized that the relationship between pvWML and impairment of cognition is caused, at least in part, by the damage of cholinergic projections from the [basal forebrain](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/basal-forebrain) to the cortex (,, ). Support for this hypothesis stems from studies showing a positive effect of cholinergic stimulation on cognition in vascular dementia (, ) and a negative relationship between pvWML and cerebral [acetylcholinesterase](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/acetylcholinesterase) (AChE) activity, a marker for the integrity of the cholinergic system, in cognitively normal elderly persons (). While in vivo measurements of AChE using PET have provided evidence for a cholinergic deficit in AD and MCI (), as well as correlations between AChE and cognition in AD (, ) and MCI (), the contribution of WML to these effects remains to be elucidated.

Accordingly, we here explored the relationship between pvWML and non-pvWML and AChE in individuals with MCI and cognitively normal elderly. We furthermore aimed to delineate common and differential effects of AChE activity and WML on cognition. Based on the above-mentioned evidence, we hypothesized that specifically the amount of pvWML would be negatively correlated with levels of AChE activity and cognitive performance.

## 2\. Methods

### 2.1. Participants

A total of 20 patients diagnosed with [MCI](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/mild-cognitive-impairment) in the Memory Clinic Cologne-Jülich (11 male, aged 54 to 80, mean 68.7 ± 6.8 y), without neurologic or psychiatric comorbidities, were recruited as part of the MACS study (Memory, Aging, and the Cholinergic System, EudraCT No. 2008-008896-32). The study consisted of an imaging characterization phase and a subsequent pharmacologic challenge phase. The imaging characterization phase contained MR and cholinergic [PET imaging](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/positron-emission-tomography) (for details, see below). During the subsequent pharmacologic challenge phase, from which no data are reported here, participants received 3-mg [rivastigmine](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/rivastigmine) or placebo on 2 separate occasions in a double-blind cross-over design. Since all subjects received the trial medication after the data presented here were obtained, trial medication had no influence on the present findings. The study was approved by the ethics committee of the medical faculty of the University of Cologne as well as the local and federal authorities. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before the study.

A total of 2 MCI patients had to be excluded as outliers based on extreme WML with confluent [leukoaraiosis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/leukoaraiosis) and a third subsequently withdrew consent. The remaining participants all met the criteria for MCI due to [AD](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/alzheimers-disease) as described by. Neuronal injury was operationalized as medial temporal atrophy assessed by an investigator blinded to clinical data (AM) using the medial temporal atrophy scale () on high-resolution T1-weighted images, temporoparietal and precuneal hypometabolism in fluorodeoxyglucose-PET quantified with 3D-SSP (), or elevated Tau-protein in cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) (>375 pg/mL). Positive CSF amyloid pathology was defined as amyloid ß <sub>1-42</sub> (Aβ) <550 pg/mL or a ratio of Tau-protein/amyloid-ß <sub>1-42</sub> >0.52 (). CSF was obtained by [lumbar puncture](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/lumbar-puncture) using a 22-gauge needle and collected in 13-mL polypropylene tubes (Sarstedt, Nümbrecht, Germany). Part of the CSF was used to assess the number of leukocytes, the number of erythrocytes, the glucose level, and total protein. The remaining CSF was centrifuged at 1600 g for 10 minutes, transferred to new polypropylene tubes, and stored at −80 °C until biomarker analysis (which was performed within a week). Amyloid-ß <sub>1-42</sub> (Aß), total tau, and phosphorylated tau were measured with commercially available [ELISAs](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/elisa) (Innotest ß-amyloid\[1-42\], Innotest hTAU-Ag and Innotest Phospho-Tau\[181P\]; Fujirebio Europe, Ghent, Belgium). One subject who presented with a typical clinical presentation of MCI due to AD showed signs of neuronal injury but just missed the cutoff for CSF Aß-pathology (Tau-protein/Aß <sub>1-42</sub> = 0.49, Aß <sub>1-42</sub> <741 pg/mL). Thus, this subject was classified as an intermediate likelihood of MCI due to AD. The other 16 patients had signs of neuronal injury and an AD-typical CSF biomarker profile, corresponding to a high likelihood of MCI due to AD (). Biomarkers for all patients are summarized in. As a control group, 22 cognitively normal older adults (14 male, aged 53 to 80, mean 66.6 ± 7.1 years) without a history of neurologic or psychiatric disease and without cholinergic, anticholinergic, or other [psychoactive drugs](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/psychoactive-drug) were recruited from the community. A total of 4 cognitively normal older participants were not eligible for further analyses due to incidental pathologic findings on MRI (1; severe cerebral atrophy), severe dental metal artifacts in MRI (2), and PET acquisition failure due to technical issues (1). Thus, the data of 18 controls and 17 MCI patients were available for further analysis. All participants underwent a physical and neurologic examination by a neurologist (N. R., J. K.).

### 2.2. Neuropsychologic assessment

A comprehensive neuropsychologic assessment was performed. This included the Mini-Mental Status Exam (MMSE), the [Beck Depression Inventory](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/beck-depression-inventory) V (), the Bayer Activities of Daily Living (), the Trail Making Test A and B (), Brief Test of Attention (), the Leistungsprüfungs system part 4 (logical thinking) (), the Verbal Learning and Memory Test (VLMT); the German version of the Rey Auditory Learning Test (), and the Rey-Osterrieth Complex Figure Test (ROCF) (). This comprehensive neuropsychologic profile also served to exclude depression or any significant impairment in the activities of daily living. For further details regarding the neuropsychologic assessment, cf.. In short, MCI was defined as a performance more than 1.5 standard deviations below the norm in the delayed recall of the VLMT, and a score of more than 24 points in the MMSE, as well as normal scores in tests of attention and logical thinking. Mnestic function was operationalized using the performance in the fifth (final) free recall repetition during [word list learning](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/word-list-recall) of the VLMT, the delayed recalls of the VLMT, and the ROCF (). The VLMT probes verbal memory, whereas the ROCF reflects visual memory but also visuoconstruction and, to a lesser degree, executive functions. Part A of the Trail Making Test (TMT) () was used as a measure of processing speed and executive functions. The TMT-B was not included in any analysis since data were not available from all participants.

### 2.3. Data acquisition

\[<sup>11</sup> C\]N-methyl-4-piperidyl acetate (MP4A) was synthesized as previously described (, ), with minor modifications. Between 192 and 556 MBq of MP4A were injected intravenously as bolus. Scanning was performed using an ECAT HRRT scanner (CPS Innovations, Knoxville TN, USA) and a protocol described by Haense et al (). High-resolution T1-weighted images (MDEFT3D; repetition time (TR) 1930 ms; inversion time (TI) 650 ms; echo time (TE) 5.8 ms; flip angle 18°; 128 sagittal slices; resolution 1.0 × 1.0 × 1.25 mm <sup>3</sup>), T2-weighted images (TR 3200 ms; TE 458 ms; 176 sagittal slices; resolution 1.0 × 1.0 × 1.0 mm <sup>3</sup>), and fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR) images (TR 8040 ms; TI 2400 ms; TE 121 ms; flip angle 150°; 36 axial slices; slice thickness 4 mm, in-plane resolution, 0.46875 × 0.46875 mm <sup>2</sup>) were acquired using a 3T Trio scanner (Siemens, Erlangen, Germany). The average time between the MR and PET measurements was 31 days (standard deviation 35 days).

### 2.4. Data processing

[AChE](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/acetylcholinesterase) activity was assessed through voxel-wise estimation of the hydrolysis rate k3 of MP4A, using a 3-parameter compartment model (, ) implemented in the software VINCI (version 4.20, Max Planck Institute for Metabolism Research, Cologne, Germany). The tracer hydrolysis rate in tissue k3 is highly correlated to AChE activity reported in postmortem studies (). The resulting k3-maps were coregistered to the T1-images and normalized to the Montreal Neurological Institute (MNI) space, using the parameters generated in the voxel-based morphometry (VBM) analysis described below. Spatial smoothing was applied to normalized k3-images using a Gaussian kernel of 8 mm (FWHM) to account for anatomical variations as well as registration and normalization errors. Subsequent statistical analyses were constrained to cortical gray matter, as k3 estimation in [subcortical structures](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/subcortical-structure) is unreliable (, ). Voxel-wise gray matter density maps modulated only for nonlinear normalization were generated using VBM implemented in the software VBM 8 ([http://dbm.neuro.uni-jena.de/vbm/](http://dbm.neuro.uni-jena.de/vbm/)). Gray matter density maps were only modulated for nonlinear normalization; therefore, intracranial volume was not entered in the analysis. Since the gray matter images differed from the k3-images in spatial resolution, they were smoothed with a Gaussian kernel of 8.24 mm (FWHM) to obtain the same final resolution in both modalities.

### 2.5. Quantifying WML

The amount of WML was rated on T2 and FLAIR images using the semiquantitative scale of Scheltens () by an investigator (AM), who was blinded to any clinical and PET data. The scale defines pvWML as WML located immediately adjacent to the [lateral ventricles](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/lateral-ventricle) (frontal and occipital caps, lateral bands) rated from 0 to 2 points in each location. Non-pvWML were defined as located deep in the frontal, parietal, temporal, or [occipital lobe](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/occipital-lobe), and rated from 0 to 6 points in each location.

### 2.6. Voxel-wise analyses

A voxel-wise multiple regression between semiquantitative ratings of WML and cortical k3 was performed using SPM8 (Statistical parametric mapping software, Wellcome Department of Imaging [Neuroscience](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/neuroscience), London, UK; [http://www.fil.ion.ucl.ac.uk](http://www.fil.ion.ucl.ac.uk/)) and the Biological Parametric Mapping toolbox ([http://fmri.wfubmc.edu/software/BPM](http://fmri.wfubmc.edu/software/BPM)) to incorporate age and VBM maps as covariates of no interest. Resulting statistical maps were thresholded at *p* < 0.001 to form clusters considered significant at an FWE-corrected cluster *p* < 0.05 level. VBM and k3 values were extracted from the area showing a significant relationship between k3 and pvWML to correlate them with neuropsychologic performance.

### 2.7. Voxel-based lesion AChE mapping

As a confirmatory approach, voxel-based lesion AChE mapping was used to locate WML exerting an effect on the cholinergic system. WML were manually traced on the T2-images to generate WML masks. T2-images were coregistered to the T1-images and the resulting coregistration parameters were applied to the WML masks, which were then normalized to MNI space using the normalization parameters of the VBM analysis. Normalized WML masks were entered into a voxel-based lesion cerebral AChE activity mapping analysis as implemented in the software MRIcron ([http://www.mccauslandcenter.sc.edu/mricro/mricron/](http://www.mccauslandcenter.sc.edu/mricro/mricron/)). The k3 of MP4A averaged across all areas showing a significant negative correlation between k3 and pvWML (as quantified using the Scheltens scale) was entered as a predictor. Results passing a false detection rate (FDR)-threshold of *p* < 0.05 are reported as significant.

### 2.8. Correlation of imaging data with cognitive performance

Correlations of imaging data with cognitive performance were assessed using SPSS (Version 22.0, IBM Corp., Armonk, NY). For normally distributed data, Pearson correlations were computed and group differences were assessed using independent samples T-tests; otherwise, Mann-Whitney and Spearman coefficients were used.

To detect the impact of AChE activity and WML load on cognition, multiple regression analyses with [neuropsychologic test](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/neuropsychological-test) scores as the dependent variable, and AChE activity, pvWML, and gray matter density as independent variables were performed. AChE activity and gray matter density were extracted from regions that had shown a significant association with pvWML load in the voxel-wise analysis. To separate the effects of gray matter atrophy, pvWML, and AChE on cognition and to justify the analysis of WML effects on condition, we tested whether WML explained variance in neuropsychologic data beyond that explained by atrophy. For this purpose, a hierarchical multiple regression was performed starting with the predictors age and gray matter and sequentially adding pvWML and AChE. To account for the non-normal distribution of the neuropsychologic test scores, robust linear regression using bootstrapping was performed. All multiple regression models were tested for collinearity. The lowest tolerance observed was 0.326, indicating that the analysis was unlikely to be biased by collinearity ().

### 2.9. Mediation and moderation analyses

Mediation analysis was performed among all participants to examine the extent to which AChE mediates the effect of WML on cognition, that is, whether pvWML affected cognition through alterations of AChE (). Mediation describes a situation where the effect of a predictor variable on an outcome variable can be explained through their relationship to a third variable, that is, the mediator: the predictor affects the mediator, which in turn affects the outcome variable. Mediation is considered present when this indirect effect is statistically significant. Furthermore, to determine whether the relationship between AChE and cognition was influenced by the amount of pvWML, a moderation analysis was performed. Regression, mediation, and moderation analyses were performed with 5000 bootstrapping samples, and bias-corrected 95% confidence intervals (BCI) are reported.

## 3\. Results

### 3.1. Demographics and WML ratings

Groups did not differ with respect to age (*p* = 0.3) or gender (*p* = 0.89). MCI participants performed significantly worse than controls in tests of memory function and the TMT-A (). Assessed with the Scheltens scale, the amount of pvWML (controls 1.7 ± 1.5, MCI 2.9 ± 1.1) differed significantly between groups (*p* = 0.033, after Bonferroni correction). In contrast, the amount of total WML (controls 5.3 ± 3.2, MCI 8.2 ± 4.9) and non-pvWML (controls 3.5 ± 2.6, MCI 5.2 ± 4.0) did not differ significantly between groups. Age was not correlated with any measures of WML.

### 3.2. Group difference in cortical AChE activity

Across the entire [cerebral cortex](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/cerebral-cortex), controls had significantly higher average AChE activity than MCI patients (controls: 0.064 ± 0.006 and MCI: 0.056 ± 0.006, *p* < 0.0001). When corrected for atrophy, MCI patients showed significantly lower AChE activity than cognitively normal participants in the temporal lobes, the left frontal lobe, as well as in bilateral inferior parietal and occipital areas (, ).

### 3.3. Correlation between WML and AChE activity

Voxel-wise analyses, including both groups and correcting for age and gray matter atrophy, revealed a significant negative correlation between k3 and pvWML load in both temporal and frontal lobes as well as the right [parietal lobe](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/parietal-lobe) (, ). A confirmatory partial correlation analysis with k3 of AChE averaged over these areas of interest and pvWML showed that this negative correlation was present across groups (Spearman correlation coefficient r = −0.7, *p* < 0.001, 95% BCI \[−0.837, −0.530\]) and within each group (controls: r = −0.52, *p* = 0.039, 95% BCI \[−0.826, −0.081\]; MCI patients: r = −0.682, *p* = 0.005, 95% BCI \[−0.923, −0.316\]). AChE did not correlate with total WML or non-pvWML.

### 3.4. Correlation between WML and neuropsychologic data

In the whole sample, a significant negative correlation was found between total WML and the [ROCF](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/rey-osterrieth-complex-figure) (r = −0.35; *p* = 0.042). pvWML were negatively correlated with the ROCF delayed recall (r = −0.44; *p* = 0.009), VLMT learning (r = −0.35; *p* = 0.042), and the TMT-A (r = 0.34; *p* = 0.048), but not the VLMT delayed recall (r = −0.25, *p* = 0.14). No correlations were observed with non-pvWML. Within groups, WML and cognitive performance were not correlated.

### 3.5. Multiple regression and mediation analyses

Multiple regression analyses revealed that AChE activity best predicted neuropsychologic performance, explaining more variance in the data than pvWML, gray matter density, or age (). After Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons, AChE activity was significantly associated with the delayed recall performance in the VLMT and the ROCF, even when correcting for gray matter atrophy, periventricular lesion load, and age. This effect was not significant for the final learning trial of the VLMT and the TMT-A. There was an overall (indirect) effect of pvWML on verbal memory learning (β = −0.32, 95% BCI \[−0.67, −0.07\]) and on visual memory (β = −0.40, 95% BCI \[−0.64, −0.19\]), which was mediated through AChE activity, but there was no direct effect of pvWML on measures of memory. Data thus suggest that AChE activity mediated the effect of pvWML on verbal memory learning and on visual memory performance (), when adjusting for age and local gray matter atrophy. Verbal memory delayed recall and TMT performance were not significantly related to pvWML in the mediation analyses. A moderating effect of pvWML on the relationship between AChE and [neuropsychologic test](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/neuropsychological-test) performance could not be detected.

## 4\. Discussion

Data suggest that pvWML contribute to mnestic deficits by disrupting cholinergic projections to the [cerebral cortex](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/cerebral-cortex). We observed that pvWML and not non-pvWML negatively influence memory performance, and that this effect is likely to be mediated via a reduction of cortical AChE activity. Overall, compared with pvWML, age, and gray matter density, AChE activity as a marker for the integrity of the cholinergic system was the best predictor of mnestic functions.

### 4.1. pvWML are negatively associated with AChE

Voxel-wise correlations between semiquantitative ratings of WML load and AChE activity showed that only pvWML, but not non-pvWML or the total amount of WML, were related to AChE activity. For the first time, we here show this in a group of patients with MCI due to AD. Thus, our data extend previous similar findings in cognitively normal older subjects (). It has been argued that only pvWML should be related to AChE activity because of their strategic location in areas with large numbers of [cholinergic fibers](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/cholinergic-fiber) (, ). On their way from the [basal forebrain](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/basal-forebrain), cholinergic projections are initially tightly packed in bundles, fanning out toward the cortex after passing the basal ganglia and ventricles. Thus, in proximity to the ventricles, their density is much higher than in the deep white matter (). The spatial distribution of cholinergic fiber bundles also provides an explanation for the fact that AChE activity in the medial temporal lobe differs between groups, but is not correlated with pvWML load, since the lower part of the lateral cholinergic pathway projecting to the medial temporal lobe does not pass through periventricular areas. Linking pvWML to AChE activity, and AChE activity to memory, our data provide a putative mechanism for the commonly observed association between pvWML and an increased risk of progression from MCI to dementia (,,, ). pvWML are likely to damage relevantly cholinergic projections to the cortex, thereby contributing to the cholinergic deficit underlying the [cognitive impairment](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/cognitive-disorders) due to AD. Consistent with this assumption, several areas showing a correlation between pvWML and AChE, namely the inferior, middle, and [superior temporal gyri](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/superior-temporal-gyrus) bilaterally extending to the left insula, as well as the right [supramarginal gyrus](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/supramarginal-gyrus) extending to the right [inferior parietal lobule](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/inferior-parietal-lobule) (), have been suggested to play a specific role in [episodic memory](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/episodic-memory) (see, e.g. \[\]). For example, the inferior parietal lobule and the supramarginal gyrus, often attributed to the [default mode network](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/default-mode-network), are commonly deactivated during encoding tasks (), but activated during retrieval of episodic memory (, ). The left middle temporal gyrus on the other hand has been shown to be involved in successful encoding of words in functional MRI studies ().

### 4.2. pvWML specifically affect AChE activity and cognition

It is likely that [neurodegeneration](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/neurodegeneration) due to AD had an influence on AChE activity and on cognition in the present sample, irrespective of pvWML. In fact, a significant group difference in AChE activity, corrected for regional atrophy, was observed in the lateral temporal lobes, reaching into the left insula, the right [parahippocampal gyrus](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/parahippocampal-gyrus), the [middle frontal gyrus](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/middle-frontal-gyrus), the [cuneus](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/cuneus) bilaterally, the left [lingual gyrus](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/lingual-gyrus), and the left [precuneus](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/precuneus). This is in line with previous studies reporting a significant reduction of AChE activity in MCI, especially in temporal and occipital regions (, ). However, while areas showing decreased AChE activity in MCI overlapped with those where AChE activity was correlated with pvWML, the association between pvWML and AChE activity extended into areas where the level of AChE activity did not significantly differ between groups. Thus, data suggest that neurodegeneration due to AD and pvWML exert differential effects on AChE activity. This is corroborated by the observation in the hierarchical regression analysis that gray matter atrophy and pvWML were complementary in explaining variance in neuropsychological performance. Thus, our data suggest that neurodegeneration due to AD and pvWML exert separate effects on AChE activity and in turn on memory performance.

### 4.3. AChE probably mediates the effect of pvWML on memory

Across both groups, pvWML significantly correlated with verbal memory learning, the delayed recall on a visual memory task, and performance in the Trail Making Test. However, multiple regression analyses showed that in areas where AChE was correlated with pvWML, local AChE activity predicted memory performance better than pvWML, gray matter atrophy, or age. Furthermore, mediation analyses showed no direct effect of pvWML on any measure of neuropsychological performance, but an indirect influence of pvWML on [word list learning](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/word-list-recall) and visual memory delayed recall. This indirect influence was fully mediated through AChE activity. Mediation analyses can be valuable for the formulation of a conceptual framework by comparing relationships between variables (, ). However, in cross-sectional settings, they need to be interpreted with caution regarding causality because they lack experimental manipulation and information about changes in the variables (, ). Nevertheless, we had a clear hypothesis regarding the temporal relationship between pvWML, a consequent reduction in neurons expressing AChE and cognition. This hypothesis is supported by the spatial colocalization of periventricular lesions and cholinergic fiber bundles (). Furthermore, our data are in line with previous findings in cognitively normal older individuals and neuropathologic data (, ). While the sample size in the present study is relatively small, empirical evidence indicates that it is large enough to detect mediation effects with the bias-corrected bootstrap method we used (). We are, therefore, confident that the results of our mediation analysis provide evidence for a specific detrimental effect of pvWML on cortical AChE activity relating to mnestic deficits. Nevertheless, this finding calls for longitudinal studies including supplemental imaging modalities for further verification.

### 4.4. Cholinergic deficits and cognitive dysfunction in AD

The findings that (1) cerebral [cholinergic activity](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/cholinergic-activity) was reduced in patients suffering from AD (,, ) and (2) memory performance could be pharmacologically modulated using cholinergic and anticholinergic substances in humans and nonhuman primates (, ) led to the cholinergic hypothesis of memory dysfunction in AD: It has been proposed that cognitive impairment in AD is attributable to the degeneration of cholinergic neurons in the basal forebrain and a subsequent loss of [cholinergic neurotransmission](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/cholinergic-transmission) in the cerebral cortex (). Other studies, however, have cast doubt on this hypothesis: (1) While experimentally induced lesions of cholinergic basal forebrain structures caused learning and memory impairments in some studies, they did not in other studies (,,, ). (2) Clinical trials in patients with AD have demonstrated a significant improvement of cognition through administration of [cholinomimetics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/parasympathomimetic-drug), albeit with modest effects only (, ). (3) In MCI, the efficacy of cholinergic pharmacotherapy to date remains controversial (, ). Nonetheless, there is evidence for a role of the cholinergic system in mnestic processes (), despite the fact that initial expectations were not met, since memory impairment in AD cannot be treated as effectively via [neurotransmitter](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/neurotransmitter) replacement as, for example, motor symptoms in [Parkinson's disease](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/parkinsons-disease) ().

#### 4.5. Limitations

It is conceivable that cognition in the present sample was additionally influenced by AD-related factors which could not be controlled for such as regional hypometabolism as measured by FDG-PET. In this study, however, FDG-PET data could not be included as a covariate in the models since FDG-PET data were available only for a subset of the MCI patients and for none of the controls. Therefore, the present findings warrant verification in future studies including supplemental imaging modalities.

## 5\. Conclusion

In cognitively normal older individuals and patients with MCI due to AD, pvWML were negatively associated with cerebral AChE activity and cognition. Data suggest that this negative effect is dissociable from the effects of [neurodegeneration](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/neurodegeneration).

Thus, the well-established association between pvWML and an increased risk for progression from MCI to AD () may be related to a greater cumulative loss of cholinergic projections to the cortex in individuals with pvWML. Further research combining structural MRI and cholinergic imaging is warranted to investigate whether this translates to an enhanced benefit due to cholinergic therapy in patients with specific types of vascular (co-) pathology.
