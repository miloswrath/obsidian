## Question 1
---
The **standard error of the sample mean (SE)** measures how much the sample mean is expected to vary from the _true population mean_ if you were to take many samples of the same size from the population.
The **standard deviation (SD)**, in contrast, measures the amount of variability or dispersion of individual data points around the mean _within_ a single population or sample.

## Question 2
---
**Central Limit Theorem** proposes that as a random sample increases in size (as n approaches ∞), the sampling distribution of the sample mean approaches a normal distribution, regardless of the shape of the population distribution. The difference becomes negligible at approximately n=30n = 30n=30.

## Question 3
---

**(a)**  
You would expect the mean of the sample means to equal the population mean,  
that is:  
$$
\mu_{\bar{x}} = \mu = 30.4 \text{ mg/100 ml}
$$

**(b)**  
The standard deviation of the sample means (also called the standard error of the mean) is given by:  
$$
\sigma_{\bar{x}} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}
$$

Substituting the given values:  
$$
\sigma_{\bar{x}} = \frac{9.14}{\sqrt{25}} = \frac{9.14}{5} = 1.83 \text{ mg/100 ml}
$$
So, the standard error of the mean is **1.83 mg/100 ml**.

**(c)**
The standard error of the means is significantly lower. This is because they are two completely different metrics. The sample size of 25 is relatively close to 30 so the deviation of sample means should be quite small.

**(d)**
As you obtain more sample means, their distribution should be relatively normal.

**(e)**
**Given:**  
$\mu = 30.4$, $\sigma = 9.14$, $n = 25$, $\bar{x} = 33$

**1. Compute Standard Error:**  
$\sigma_{\bar{x}} = \frac{9.14}{\sqrt{25}} = 1.83$

**2. Compute z-score:**  
$z = \frac{33 - 30.4}{1.83} = 1.42$

**3. Find Probability:**  
$P(\bar{X} > 33) = P(Z > 1.42) = 1 - 0.9222 = 0.0778$

**Answer:**  
≈ **0.078** (or **7.8%**) of the sample means exceed 33 mg/100 ml.

**(f)**  

**Given:**  
$\mu = 30.4$, $\sigma = 9.14$, $n = 25$, so $\sigma_{\bar{x}} = 1.83$

**1. Compute z-score:**  
$z = \dfrac{28 - 30.4}{1.83} = \dfrac{-2.4}{1.83} = -1.31$

**2. Find Probability:**  
$P(\bar{X} < 28) = P(Z < -1.31) = 0.0951$

**Answer:**  
≈ **0.095** (or **9.5%**) of the sample means are less than 28 mg/100 ml.



**(g)**  
**1. Compute z-scores:**  
$z_1 = \dfrac{29 - 30.4}{1.83} = -0.77$  
$z_2 = \dfrac{31 - 30.4}{1.83} = 0.33$

**2. Find Probability:**  
$P(29 < \bar{X} < 31) = P(-0.77 < Z < 0.33)$  
$= P(Z < 0.33) - P(Z < -0.77)$  
$= 0.6293 - 0.2206 = 0.4087$

**Answer:**  
≈ **0.409** (or **40.9%**) of the sample means fall between 29 and 31 mg/100 ml.****


## Question 4
---

Given: $\mu = 1.93\ \mu g/m^3$, $\sigma = 2.18\ \mu g/m^3$, $n = 63$

By the Central Limit Theorem, the sampling distribution is **approximately normal**:

$$
\bar{X} \sim \mathcal{N}(1.93,\ (0.275)^2)
$$

where  
$$\sigma_{\bar{X}} = \dfrac{2.18}{\sqrt{63}} \approx 0.275\ \mu g/m^3$$

## Question 5
---
The width of a confidence interval depends on the confidence level ($1 - \alpha$), the sample size ($n$), and the population standard deviation ($\sigma$).

A higher confidence level means we want more certainty that the interval contains the true parameter, which increases the critical value and therefore makes the interval wider.

The standard deviation reflects how spread out the data are. Greater variability in the population leads to a wider confidence interval.

The sample size has an inverse square root relationship with interval width. Larger samples produce smaller confidence intervals because the estimate of the mean becomes more precise.
