
### **1. Normal Distribution – Blood Glucose Levels**

For adult males, fasting blood glucose levels are approximately normally distributed with a mean of 95 mg/dL and a standard deviation of 10 mg/dL.

Let $X$ represent the fasting glucose level of a randomly selected adult male.

a) What is the probability that a randomly selected man has a fasting glucose level greater than 110 mg/dL?

b) A researcher selects a random sample of 25 adult males. What is the probability that their **mean** fasting glucose level exceeds 98 mg/dL?

$P(Z>\cfrac{110-95}{10^2}) = 0.0668$ 
$P(\bar{X} > 98)= 0.0668$ same as part a

### **2. t-Distribution – Caffeine Intake**

A public health researcher is studying daily caffeine intake (in mg) among college students.  
A sample of 9 students had a mean daily caffeine intake of 210 mg with a sample standard deviation of 36 mg.  
Assume caffeine intake follows a normal distribution.

a) Construct a 95% confidence interval for the **mean daily caffeine intake** of college students.

b) Interpret this interval in context

$\mu = 210$
$dof = 8$ 
$s = 36$
