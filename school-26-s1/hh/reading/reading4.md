## Scientific article questions
1. What was the research question for this study?
	- To investigate the proportion of caregiver burnout and explore potential contributing factors to caregiver burnout in HK and NZ
	- To examine the impact of formal care service on the relationship between potential contributing factors and caregiver burnout 
2. What methods were used in the study?
	- cross-sectional study using the routinely collected data of interRAI in HK and NZ to investigate the proportion of caregiver burnout and explore potential contributing factors to caregiver burnout
	- Created a composite score of subjective and objective measures to determine caregiver burnout
3. Who were the caregivers who participated in the study?
	- Data sources consisted of a population-based cohort of all older adult seeking support from public-funded community and/or residential care in HK and NZ1
	- Used their primary caregivers
4. Were there significant differences between the two nations in how often (or how profoundly) caregivers faced burnout?
	- Prevalence of caregiver burnout was 15.5 and 13.9% in HK and NZ respectively 
	- Care-recipients’ ADL dependency and fall history in 90 days, and primary caregiver living with care-recipients were significantly associated with caregiver burnout in the NZ population only. In the HK population, primary caregiver being child or child-in-law, and the utilization of home care services and hospital service were significantly associated with additional risk of caregiver burnout.
5. What cultural differences were observed that contributed to, or helped to reduce, caregiver burnout in these two countries?
	- There were more people living alone in the NZ sample than that in HK, which suggests cohabitation maybe unusual in NZ and could bring additional stress to caregivers. 
	- Living in the same environment with the care-recipients could bring increased chance for conflicts and even abusive behaviours to the caregiving dyad, and therefore may give more emotional burden to the caregivers of people with dementia.
	- Another difference identified was that the ADL dependence of care-recipient had a significant association with caregiver burnout in NZ sample only, despite the fact that there are higher proportions of ADL-dependent care-recipients and more primary caregiving providing ADL care in HK. This could be explained by the difference in cultural values in the two populations. The concept of filial piety and the caregiving responsibility deeply rooted in the culture of the Chinese may be responsible for the high percentage of ADL care provision in HK.


> Explanation of second finding about ADL dependence
### 1. Finding 
The study found that **Activities of Daily Living (ADL) dependence** of the care recipient was significantly associated with **caregiver burnout** in the New Zealand (NZ) sample **only**, even though:

- **Hong Kong (HK)** had **more ADL-dependent care recipients** (meaning more older adults who need help with basic tasks like bathing, eating, or toileting), and
    
- HK caregivers were **more likely to provide ADL care** than NZ caregivers.
    

This is counterintuitive—one might expect burnout to be higher in the group (HK) where more caregivers provide ADL care. But the association was stronger in NZ.

---

### 2. **Cultural Explanation**

The researchers attribute this difference to **cultural values**, particularly:

- **Filial piety (孝)**: A Confucian value in Chinese culture that emphasizes children’s duty to care for their aging parents.
    
- **Collectivism**: The belief that family needs outweigh personal needs, encouraging sacrifice for the well-being of elders.
    

In Hong Kong, these values create **strong social norms**:

- Providing ADL care is seen not only as a responsibility but as a moral obligation.
    
- Caregivers may view these tasks—even unpleasant ones like washing or toileting—as an expected, honorable part of family life.
    
- As a result, **HK caregivers may feel more emotionally prepared and more tolerant** of the burdens of ADL care, reducing the link between ADL dependence and burnout.
