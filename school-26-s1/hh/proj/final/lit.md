
## 1. Problem specification: what’s wrong with autobiographical memory in aging?

Key pattern: with age (and especially with MCI and Alzheimer’s), people show **reduced autobiographical memory specificity** and more **overgeneral memories** (broad, vague, non-specific).

- Healthy cognitive aging and dementia both show declines in specific episodic autobiographical recall, with a shift toward semantic/factual self-knowledge and overgeneral memories. ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1568163717302465?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Review Autobiographical memory, the ageing brain and ..."))
    
- In Alzheimer’s disease, autobiographical memory deficits include **reduced detail**, **overgeneralization**, and a temporal gradient: older, remote memories are relatively preserved while recent ones are impaired. ([PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12207997/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Autobiographical memory in Alzheimer's disease: a systematic ..."))
    
- Overgeneral autobiographical memory in older adults is linked to difficulties generating specific events in response to cues, compared with younger adults. ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317160319_Overgeneral_autobiographical_memory_and_depression_in_older_adults_a_systematic_review?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Overgeneral autobiographical memory and depression in ..."))
    

So the target problem you can name is something like:

> **Problem:** Older adults (especially those with MCI or dementia) increasingly retrieve vague, overgeneral autobiographical memories with reduced specificity and detail, undermining their sense of self, emotional regulation, and everyday decision-making.

## 2. Effects of this problem on the individual and their surroundings

### Effects on the individual

Autobiographical memory is tightly tied to **identity, emotion, and cognition**:

- Autobiographical memory specificity is associated with **cognitive function, depression, and independence** in older adults; poorer specificity predicts worse mood and functional outcomes. ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.652600/full?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Specific Autobiographical Recall Mediates Impact of ..."))
    
- In Alzheimer’s disease, autobiographical memory impairment affects multiple domains and contributes to a **weakened sense of self** and altered emotional processing (e.g., positivity bias in recall but with less detail). ([PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12207997/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Autobiographical memory in Alzheimer's disease: a systematic ..."))
    
- Overgeneral autobiographical memory is linked to **late-life depression and poorer social problem-solving**, suggesting it’s not just neutral decline but a risk factor for emotional disorders. ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317160319_Overgeneral_autobiographical_memory_and_depression_in_older_adults_a_systematic_review?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Overgeneral autobiographical memory and depression in ..."))
    

Downstream consequences you can articulate:

- Blurred life narrative → fragile or “thinned” identity.
    
- Less access to specific positive memories → vulnerability to depression/anxiety.
    
- Harder time using past experiences to guide **future planning** and **problem-solving**.
    
- Reduced sense of continuity (“Who am I?” / “How did I get here?”).
    

### Effects on surroundings (caregivers, family, clinical context)

- Systematic and umbrella reviews of **Life Story Book** interventions in dementia show that impaired autobiographical memory hurts **communication, relationships, and care quality**; when AM is supported, communication and relationship quality improve. ([Cambridge University Press & Assessment](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/D1FC858652BD40FC5551356474AEC368/S1041610218000376a.pdf/life_story_books_for_people_with_dementia_a_systematic_review.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Life story books for people with dementia"))
    
- Digital life storybook work in care homes reports that when autobiographical details are accessible, **staff better understand residents**, can personalize care, and manage behavioral symptoms more effectively. ([Dove Medical Press](https://www.dovepress.com/article/download/28955?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Digital life storybooks for people with dementia living in ..."))
    

So you can frame environmental effects as:

- Families and staff lose “access” to the person’s story → interactions become task-focused and shallow.
    
- Less shared history in conversations → more loneliness and withdrawal.
    
- Staff with limited knowledge of the person’s life rely more on **routine and control** than on individualized, person-centered care.

## 4. How can a humanities-based intervention help? (Mechanistically & emotionally)

### Example intervention “type”

You can bundle these under **narrative memory strengthening** in a humanities frame:

- **Life Review / Reminiscence Therapy**: guided recall of life periods (childhood, work, family) often with photos, music, or objects. ([PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6494367/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Reminiscence therapy for dementia - PMC - PubMed Central"))
    
- **Life Story Books / Digital Life Storybooks**: co-created narrative artifacts (books, videos, digital apps) that organize a person’s life into chapters with text, pictures, and captions. ([Cambridge University Press & Assessment](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/D1FC858652BD40FC5551356474AEC368/S1041610218000376a.pdf/life_story_books_for_people_with_dementia_a_systematic_review.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Life story books for people with dementia"))
    
- **Autobiographical Memory Specificity / Flexibility Training with narrative content** (e.g., MemFlex, MeST adapted to older adults): repeated practice retrieving specific, event-level memories in narrative form. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29733760/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Memory flexibility training for autobiographical ..."))

### 4a. Mechanistic (neuroscience / cognitive) pathways

From the aging-neuroscience side, the mechanisms you can lean on:

1. **Reactivation and strengthening of hippocampal–cortical networks.**
    
    - Autobiographical memory relies on hippocampus, medial prefrontal cortex, posterior cingulate, and the wider **default mode network (DMN)**. Reviews link AM, aging, and DMN function. ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1568163717302465?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Review Autobiographical memory, the ageing brain and ..."))
        
    - Repeated, structured retrieval (as in reminiscence or life review) likely promotes **reconsolidation and strengthening** of remaining memory traces, especially for emotionally salient events.
        
2. **Increased memory specificity and flexibility.**
    
    - Interventions like memory specificity/flexibility training in older adults improved **specific event recall**, cognitive flexibility, and aspects of emotional memory. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29733760/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Memory flexibility training for autobiographical ..."))
        
    - Reminiscence protocols such as REMPOS (reminiscence program) improved **specific positive memory recall**, cognitive functioning, and reduced depressive symptoms in cognitively impaired older adults. ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/10/14/3168?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Improvement of the Quality of Life in Aging by Stimulating ..."))
        
3. **Better top-down control over emotional recall.**
    
    - Poor autobiographical memory is a marker and predictor of late-life depression; improving AM specificity is hypothesized to **disrupt overgeneral, ruminative memory styles** and provide more balanced emotional recall. ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.652600/full?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Specific Autobiographical Recall Mediates Impact of ..."))
        
4. **Scaffolding semantic + episodic self-knowledge.**
    
    - Life Story Books and similar tools integrate **semantic facts** (dates, roles) with **episodic scenes**, providing an external scaffold that helps people access episodic memories they might no longer freely retrieve. ([Cambridge University Press & Assessment](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/D1FC858652BD40FC5551356474AEC368/S1041610218000376a.pdf/life_story_books_for_people_with_dementia_a_systematic_review.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Life story books for people with dementia"))
        

Mechanistic soundbite you can use:

> By repeatedly cueing and organizing autobiographical memories in narrative form, humanities-based interventions reactivate hippocampal–DMN networks, increase event-level specificity, and provide external narrative scaffolds that support identity and emotion regulation.

### 4b. Emotional & relational pathways

The humanities piece lets you talk about **meaning, identity, and relationships**, not just performance on memory tests:

- **Identity and self-continuity.**  
    Life story work and reminiscence are consistently reported to strengthen **sense of self, self-integrity, pride, and self-esteem** in people with dementia. ([ASHA Apps](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/9848644e-be55-ef11-8151-005056834e2b?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Summary of the Systematic Review"))
    
- **Mood and well-being.**  
    Systematic reviews and RCTs show reminiscence-based interventions can reduce **depressive symptoms**, improve **life satisfaction**, and enhance **quality of life** in cognitively impaired older adults. ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/10/14/3168?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Improvement of the Quality of Life in Aging by Stimulating ..."))
    
- **Communication & relationships.**  
    Life Story Books and digital storybooks improve **quantity and quality of interactions** between residents and caregivers/family; staff report better understanding and more effective strategies for managing distress and behavior. ([Cambridge University Press & Assessment](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/D1FC858652BD40FC5551356474AEC368/S1041610218000376a.pdf/life_story_books_for_people_with_dementia_a_systematic_review.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Life story books for people with dementia"))
    
- **Person-centered care culture.**  
    Narrative artifacts pull staff away from seeing someone as “a dementia patient in room 204” toward a **whole person with a history**, which shifts the culture of care in the environment. ([PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4239434/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Life story resources in dementia care: a review - PMC"))
    

A nice one-line bridge:

> Humanities-based autobiographical interventions don’t only improve what people can recall; they change **how they feel about their lives**, how others see them, and how care is delivered in the spaces where they age.
