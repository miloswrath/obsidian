## **1. The Kidnapping / First Personality Shift in the Car**

**Neutral Viewer Reaction:**  
Confusion, fear, tension—the shift seems unpredictable and dangerous.

**How This Damages Healing / Education:**

- Links DID with violence, unpredictability, and criminal behavior.
    
- Reinforces the false belief that people with DID are dangerous.
    
- Undermines real individuals’ attempts to seek support and treatment because the condition is framed as frightening.
    

**Better Way to Educate the Public:**  
Show DID episodes without pairing them with violence—e.g., depict daily struggles, grounding strategies, or how people manage identity shifts with a therapist’s help.

## **2. Final Escape Sequence (Casey Seeing Kevin’s Flashbacks)**

**Neutral Viewer Reaction:**  
Sympathy, shock, emotional intensity—this is one of the few glimpses into his past.

**How This Damages Healing:**  
The movie uses trauma **only** to justify violence rather than explain it.  
It reinforces: “Trauma makes you dangerous.”

**Why It Fails to Educate:**  
The film never highlights how trauma survivors can heal, nor that most people with DID are nonviolent and are _far more likely to be victims_ than perpetrators.

**Better Education Approach:**  
Contrast the film with real recovery models:  
– trauma-processing therapy  
– safe relationships  
– coping skills  
– stabilizing environments  
This shifts the narrative from “trauma breeds monsters” to “trauma requires compassion and evidence-based care.”
## **3. Dr. Fletcher’s Office Scene (Therapist Scene)**

**Neutral Viewer Reaction:**  
Interest in the professional viewpoint; slight disbelief at her explanations.

**How This Damages Healing / Education:**

- Although the therapist attempts to educate, the script exaggerates DID’s abilities (e.g., different physical powers, biological extremes).
    
- She unintentionally legitimizes the film’s supernatural portrayal by calling DID “a potential for greatness,” which isn’t supported by research.
    
- Seeds misinformation under the guise of authority—making viewers think DID grants superhuman traits.
    

**Better Way to Educate the Public:**  
Have the therapist emphasize grounding, coping, the role of trauma, and current evidence-based treatment.  
Avoid framing DID as “evolutionary potential.”  
Highlight safety, stability work, and real therapeutic challenges.

## **4. Transformation Into "The Beast"**

**Neutral Viewer Reaction:**  
Shock, horror, adrenaline—feels like a monster reveal.

**How This Damages Healing / Education:**

- Promotes the idea that DID can create physically enhanced, violent beings.
    
- Pushes DID into supernatural/horror territory, making it impossible for the public to see it as a real clinical disorder.
    
- Deepens stigma and fear, making treatment-seeking harder for real patients.
    

**Better Way to Educate the Public:**  
Portray trauma responses realistically.  
Use symbolic metaphors rather than literal monsters.  
Show resilience, therapy breakthroughs, and community understanding instead of framing the disorder as a threat.
